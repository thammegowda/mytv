Attention

Attention is rarely lost in a single moment. It is given away in handfuls.

The next evening Mara returned to the chair by the window with a book. She had owned it for six years and had begun it four times. Each attempt was marked by a different receipt, folded and forgotten between the early chapters.

She opened to the first page again.

The opening paragraph was not difficult, but she read it twice. Her mind kept leaning toward the bowl near the door. The phone was silent, which somehow made it louder. Its possible messages gathered weight precisely because she could not see them.

Mara put one finger beneath the line and continued.

After a page, the imagined messages weakened. After three pages, the street outside entered the story as rain. After five, she no longer remembered to measure how long she had been reading.

This was not concentration as she had pictured it. There was no locked door in the mind, no heroic refusal of distraction. There was only the small act of returning: to the next sentence, to a name, to the shape of a thought unfolding across the page.

Attention is not a possession. It is a practice of return.

Sometimes she returned every minute. Sometimes the story carried her for ten. The measure was not how rarely she wandered, but how gently she came back.

Halfway through the chapter, a phrase stopped her. She read it aloud. The words sounded different in the room, less like information and more like company. She repeated them once, then continued.

Outside, windows brightened one by one. Each held a separate life: dinner, music, an argument, a child refusing sleep. Mara felt no urge to know their details. It was enough to see the lights and understand that every illuminated square contained a world larger than it appeared from across the street.

When she closed the book, the phone still waited in the bowl. There were messages. None had become urgent through waiting. She answered one, postponed another, and deleted two notifications without opening them.

The receipt marking her old stopping place remained many chapters ahead. For the first time, it looked reachable.
